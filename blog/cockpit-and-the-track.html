The Cockpit and the Track Run on the Same Operating System

By Dawson Gant  /  November 11, 2025

People ask why I fly and race when I already run multiple businesses. Because both force a standard I cannot fake.

I hold a private pilot license and fly as often as the calendar allows. I race competitively. I play a public course in Scottsdale most mornings before the day starts. These are not escapes from the work. They are part of how I operate.

What flying teaches you about business

In a cockpit at altitude, there is no room for performative confidence. You read instruments, manage risk, and make decisions with incomplete data while staying calm. That is also what it feels like when fulfillment volume spikes, a contractor no-shows on a rehab, or a compliance review lands on short notice.

What racing adds

Racing compresses feedback. You feel instantly when your line is wrong, when you pushed too early, or when you hesitated and lost the window. Business feedback loops are slower, but the discipline is identical: prepare obsessively, execute cleanly, debrief honestly.

  • Licensed private pilot — regular flight as part of operating rhythm, not a vanity credential
  • Competitive racing — high-consequence decisions on compressed timelines
  • Daily golf in Scottsdale — structure and reset before high-stakes operating days

The founders I respect are not the ones who never step away. They are the ones who train attention somewhere rigorous enough that the boardroom cannot make them sloppy.

Dawson Gant is a licensed private pilot, competitive race car driver, and Scottsdale operator. He has built and flipped over a hundred homes, scaled telehealth to seven figures in four months, and leads a team of more than fifty employees.
